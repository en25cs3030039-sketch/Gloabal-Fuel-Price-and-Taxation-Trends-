Project Objectives & Purpose

Energy pricing is a foundational economic driver influencing consumer inflation, logistics overhead, industrial productivity, and environmental policy. The overarching objectives of this project include:

  • Demystify Price Discrepancies: Examine why fuel prices vary by hundreds of percent across nations despite tapping the same crude barrels.
  • Quantify Taxation Burdens: Analyze the proportion of pump prices attributable to excise duties, value-added taxes (VAT), and carbon levies.
  • Examine Subsidy Regimes: Map sovereign energy subsidies and evaluate their resilience against Brent crude market shocks.
  • Provide Interactive Exploration: Deliver a modern web portal and Tableau dashboard allowing policymakers, analysts, and students to investigate trends dynamically.

Dataset Source & Field Dictionary

Standardized Metrics

The underlying analytical dataset consolidates global energy price monitoring sources, World Bank economic classifications, and international benchmark spot prices. All fuel price measures are normalized to USD per Liter to enable fair cross-border comparability.

Field Name Data Type Domain / Units Description & Analytical Relevance
Country String / Geo ISO Country Names Geographic entity used for choropleth mapping and spatial price distribution.
Date Date YYYY-MM / Daily / Monthly Temporal observation point for tracking price fluctuations and trend lines.
Income Level Categorical High, Upper-Middle, Lower-Middle, Low World Bank economic classification based on Gross National Income (GNI) per capita.
Region Categorical Continental Regional Groups Continental grouping (e.g. Europe, North America, Asia-Pacific, Latin America, MENA, Africa).
Subsidy Level Categorical High, Moderate, None, Negative Tax Categorization of government market interventions that lower consumer pump prices.
Brent Crude USD Float USD per Barrel ($/bbl) The premier international benchmark price for light sweet crude oil extracted from the North Sea.
Diesel USD Liter Float USD ($) per Liter Retail pump price for automotive and commercial transport diesel fuel.
LPG USD Liter Float USD ($) per Liter Equivalent Retail price for Liquefied Petroleum Gas, vital for household energy and clean cooking.
Petrol USD Liter Float USD ($) per Liter Retail pump price for consumer motor gasoline (unleaded petrol).
Tax Percentage Float / Percent Percentage (%) of Retail Price Estimated total fiscal burden (excise duties, carbon taxes, VAT/GST) as a percentage of total price.
Pipeline & Transformation

Data Preparation Methodology

1. Cleaning & Imputation

Audited raw records for anomalies, removed duplicate national reporting dates, and addressed missing values using regional interpolation where appropriate.

2. Normalization

Converted diverse local currencies (EUR, INR, JPY, GBP, etc.) and volume units (gallons vs liters) into standardized USD per Liter using official exchange rates.

3. Dimension Enrichment

Joined geographical geographic shape attributes, regional classifications, and World Bank income level metadata to empower hierarchical drill-downs.

4. Integrity Validation

Cross-verified extreme outliers (e.g. heavily subsidized pricing in Venezuela and Iran vs highest taxation in Norway and Hong Kong) against published reports.

Visualizations Created in Tableau

The Tableau workbook integrates multiple specialized chart types configured for intuitive discovery:

  • Global Choropleth Heatmap: Color-codes national fuel prices to reveal geographic patterns and regional clustering.
  • Tax Percentage vs. Retail Price Scatter: Maps the direct mathematical correlation between tax percentage and retail gasoline pricing.
  • Brent Crude Elasticity Dual-Axis Trend: Plots Brent crude spot price against local fuel retail averages over time.
  • Income Tier & Subsidy Distribution: Compares pricing dispersion across Low, Lower-Middle, Upper-Middle, and High Income groupings.

Limitations of the Analysis

In compliance with academic analytics standards, the following methodological boundaries and data limitations should be considered:

  • Exchange Rate Fluctuations: Because retail prices are captured in local currency and converted to USD, rapid currency depreciation can create apparent dollar price drops.
  • Sub-National Variance: Federal nations (e.g. United States, Canada, India) apply divergent state/provincial taxes, which are aggregated to national averages in this dataset.
  • Indirect Subsidies: Non-cash subsidies (such as upstream refining losses absorbed by state-owned oil enterprises) are difficult to fully capture as direct tax offsets.
  • Temporal Lag: Certain developing markets report pump price adjustments on monthly schedules rather than daily spot tracking.
